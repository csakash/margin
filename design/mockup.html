The quiet power of writing in the margins

By a reader who kept losing their notes · margins.example/essays/quiet-power

Every serious reader eventually discovers that the best ideas show up mid-paragraph. Not before you start, not after you finish, but right in the middle of someone else's sentence. The trick is catching them without breaking the spell of reading.

Most note tools ask you to leave. Switch apps, find the right notebook, wait for a sync spinner, and by the time the cursor blinks the thought has already gone soft. A margin never asks you to leave. It sits right next to the text, patient and empty.

Friction is the real enemy

If capturing a thought costs more than two seconds, you will capture fewer thoughts. That is not a discipline problem, it is a design problem. The tools we reach for while reading should be closer to a pencil than to a filing cabinet.

The note you take in the moment is worth ten you promise to write later.

Drag this sentence into the panel on the right and watch it become a quote with its source attached. That is the whole idea: the reading surface and the writing surface share one screen, and moving between them costs nothing.

Small, local, always there

A margin tool has to be light enough to forget about. It should cost almost nothing to keep open all day, start instantly, and keep your words on your own disk as plain files you could open in any editor ten years from now.

It should also accept the messy stuff reading produces: a screenshot of a chart, a link to a paper, a short clip, a half-finished list of things to try. None of that should require a plugin or an account.

What good looks like

You press a key, a calm pane appears above whatever you are reading, you type, you press the key again, and it folds away into a thin line at the edge of the screen. Nothing else moves. Your page is exactly where you left it.

That is a deceptively small product. Building it well means caring about window layers, memory, and the feel of typing more than about features.

Product requirements · Draft 3

Margin: a floating, local-first notepad for Mac

A small black-glass panel that floats above anything, including full-screen Chrome, so you can write, paste and drop things while you read. It has Notion-style markdown and unlimited notes, needs no account, and uses little enough memory to leave running all day.

Platform: macOS 14+Owner: youStatus: SpecUpdated: 23 Nov 2026

01Problem

When you read in a full-screen browser, taking a note means leaving the page. macOS full-screen Spaces hide every normal window, so Notes, Notion and Obsidian all force a Space switch. Each switch breaks your focus, and web-based note apps use 300–600 MB of RAM just to stay open. Ideas end up scattered across bookmarks, desktop screenshots and half-written messages to yourself.

02Goals & non-goals

Goals

  • Always within reach. One global hotkey shows the panel over any app, including full-screen Spaces. It doesn't take focus from the page underneath until you click into it.
  • Fast capture. Under 150 ms from hotkey to typing. Dropping, pasting and clipping each take one gesture.
  • Notion-style editing. Markdown shortcuts and a / menu, saved as plain .md files.
  • Unlimited local notes. No account, no cloud and no telemetry by default.
  • Featherweight. Under 40 MB of RAM and 0% CPU when idle, so it's safe to launch at login.
  • Feels premium. The panel looks like a black-glass object, and its motion follows the pointer and can be interrupted at any point.

Non-goals (v1)

  • Sync, sharing, collaboration or a web version
  • Databases, tables, nested pages, backlinks
  • iOS / iPad app, Windows or Linux
  • AI features (at most a later, opt-in, on-device add-on)

03Users

Primary
The deep reader

Reads long blogs, papers and docs in full-screen Chrome. Wants quotes, thoughts and screenshots in one place without switching Spaces.

Secondary
The builder

Watches tutorials or reads API docs while coding. Needs a floating scratchpad over the editor for snippets, links and to-dos.

Tertiary
The meeting taker

Takes notes during a full-screen call without the panel showing up in the screen share.

04Key user stories

  • As a reader in full-screen Chrome, I press ⌥ Space and a panel appears above the page, so I can write without leaving.
  • I select a sentence on the page and drag it into the panel. It becomes a quote with the page title and URL attached.
  • I press ⌃⌥ S, drag over a region, and the screenshot appears in my current note.
  • I click “Clip tab” and the URL and title of the front Chrome tab become a link card.
  • I tap “All notes” or drag up from the bottom. A drawer slides up, pushing my note up instead of covering it, and I can search, filter and switch notes.
  • I put the panel on any of 8 spots (4 edges, 4 corners) by dragging it. While I drag, the screen dims and every spot lights up, so I can see where it will land.
  • I minimise it to a thin glowing line on its edge, with no text or logo. I know it's there, but it doesn't pull my eye off the page. I click the line to open it, or drag it to another spot.

06Window behaviour spec

  • An NSPanel with .nonactivatingPanel, so showing it never pulls you out of Chrome's full-screen Space.
  • Collection behaviour .canJoinAllSpaces, .fullScreenAuxiliary, .stationary and .ignoresCycle. The window level is .floating, raised to .statusBar while another app is full screen.
  • The logo lives only in the Dock, so the app runs with the regular activation policy. If the M0 spike shows a regular app can't float over another app's full-screen Space, it switches to .accessory while a full-screen app is in front. The Dock is hidden in full-screen Spaces anyway, so nobody sees the icon disappear.
  • Other settings: hidesOnDeactivate = false, becomesKeyOnlyIfNeeded = true, and sharingType = .none while “hide on screen share” is on.
  • Acceptance test: with Chrome in full screen, pressing the hotkey shows the panel within 150 ms and Chrome stays full screen.

10Privacy & permissions

  • Fully offline. The only network call is the optional fetch of link previews.
  • Permissions are requested only when a feature first needs them: Screen Recording for screenshots, Automation for clipping a tab.
  • Sandboxed, notarised and distributed as a signed DMG, with the Mac App Store later.

12Risks & open questions

  • How windows behave over full-screen apps can change between macOS releases. Mitigation: the M0 spike on three versions, plus an automated UI test.
  • Pointer-driven light must not cost idle CPU. Track the pointer only while it's over the panel, limited to the display's frame rate.
  • Blocks in TextKit are real work. Keep the v1 block set narrow.
  • Should the drawer remember its height between sessions? Proposed: yes, per display.
  • The drag overlay covers the whole screen. It is created when a drag starts and destroyed on drop, so it costs nothing while idle.
Tech stack research

Go native: an AppKit panel, a SwiftUI interface and a TextKit 2 editor

Two requirements decide this. The panel has to float over another app's full-screen Space, and it has to cost almost nothing to leave open all day. The material and motion also need real window blur and smooth native animation. Native Swift is the only option that does all of this without workarounds.

Recommendation. An NSPanel hosts SwiftUI for the header, drawer and toolbar, and an NSTextView on TextKit 2 does the markdown editing. Core Animation layers draw the rim light, sheen and glow. Notes are stored as .md files with a GRDB + FTS5 search index. Expected idle memory is 25–45 MB.

03The panel and the drawer, in code

final class FloatingPanel: NSPanel {
  init(content: NSView) {
    super.init(contentRect: .init(x: 0, y: 0, width: 440, height: 600),
               styleMask: [.nonactivatingPanel, .titled, .resizable, .fullSizeContentView],
               backing: .buffered, defer: false)
    isFloatingPanel = true; level = .floating
    collectionBehavior = [.canJoinAllSpaces, .fullScreenAuxiliary, .stationary, .ignoresCycle]
    hidesOnDeactivate = false; becomesKeyOnlyIfNeeded = true
    titlebarAppearsTransparent = true; titleVisibility = .hidden
    backgroundColor = .clear; isOpaque = false; hasShadow = true
    contentView = content
  }
  override var canBecomeKey: Bool { true }
}

// The drawer pushes the editor up: a VStack, not an overlay
VStack(spacing: 0) {
  Header()
  MarkdownEditor(anchorBottom: true)      // keeps the caret in view while it shrinks
  Toolbar(onDrag: drawer.track)
  NotesDrawer().frame(height: drawer.height)
}
.animation(.spring(response: 0.3, dampingFraction: 0.8), value: drawer.isOpen)

04Libraries

  • KeyboardShortcuts for global hotkeys the user can rebind.
  • swift-markdown (Apple's, built on cmark-gfm) to parse and write markdown.
  • GRDB.swift for SQLite and FTS5 search.
  • System frameworks: LinkPresentation, ScreenCaptureKit, AVKit and ImageIO.
  • Sparkle for updates outside the App Store.

05Why not SwiftUI's TextEditor?

On macOS 26, TextEditor supports rich text through AttributedString, but developers report that it still can't embed images, video or checkboxes in the text. Those are central here, so the editor uses NSTextView on TextKit 2 instead.

06If you'd rather build with web skills

Use Tauri 2 with TipTap, plus tauri-nspanel to make the window a real floating panel. Everything in this mockup ports almost directly to it. You'd trade a faster editor build for more memory and GPU use. Avoid Electron.
